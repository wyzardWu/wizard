Overview

One image in. A world you can play.

Wizard keeps the game state explicit: scene geometry for the world, an articulated skeleton for every character, and rules for the fight. Agents edit that state; a causal video model paints it at 24 FPS. Every scene and character below is new to the model.

01 · Explore

Walk beyond the picture

The scene image becomes editable 3D geometry. Keys drive the skeleton, the mouse drives the camera, and the terrain extends past the edge of the input view.

02 · Summon

Any picture becomes a boss

Drop in one image of a creature. Within minutes it is segmented, lifted to a mesh, rigged, and given moves that fit its body and style. Then it walks into your world and fights, looking like its picture.

03 · Transform

Turn one thing into another

A new picture rigs a new body that keeps the old one's place, heading and health. The old identity is masked out of the cache, so the change is instant and the scene stays.

04 · Reshape

Choose how the world changes

Pick a spell. The world agent writes a short program over geometric operators, the geometry changes, and the next proxy frame carries it. Drag across the frame to compare the video with its proxy.

05 · Play by the rules

The state decides who wins

A planner picks each boss move from its own motion set; a rule-based controller checks swept hitboxes, dodges and cooldowns, and applies damage. Health, hits and the final blow come from the simulation, and the model renders them.

How it works

Agents keep the state. The model paints it.

A setup stage of under five minutes reconstructs the scene and characters, rigs skeletons and builds each character's motion set. During play, the framework updates motion, geometry and combat state, and the generator renders them.

(a)Character animation

Qwen3-VL infers each character's body structure; SAM2, TripoSG and UniRig turn it into a labelled skeleton. Motions from CombatMotion, WildWorld and Elden Ring are retrieved by compatible body structure and VLM-judged style, then retargeted limb chain by limb chain.

(b)Scene manipulation

MoGe-2 recovers geometry and camera. An OpenGL renderer draws the proxy, and Qwen3-VL-8B turns instructions into programs over geometric operators, from raising a platform to turning the world upside down.

(c)State-aware combat control

The state tracks positions, poses, health, action phases and cooldowns. The planner simulates candidate attacks against the player's geometry; the controller resolves hits, damage, reactions and knock-outs.

(d)Real-time video generation

Wan2.2-TI2V-5B renders the proxies conditioned on the first frame, one reference per character and the retained history, at 24 FPS with two denoising steps.

MaskingCharacter-aware adaptive masking

Each reference may reach only its own character's skeleton-derived region, expanded by 25%. The masks follow the characters, reduce appearance leakage between them, and on a transformation block the old identity's cached tokens while the background and first-frame sink stay visible.

SpeedCausal adaptation and distillation

Following Causal Forcing++: teacher forcing, consistency distillation with a 20-latent-frame window and a first-frame sink, then DMD on the student's own rollouts. Proxy conditions and gated references stay active throughout.
